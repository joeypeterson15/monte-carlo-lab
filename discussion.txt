

CLAIM GAP:
    If a worker pops a chunk from queue:jobs and crashes before writing its lease and adding to inflight, 
    the chunk is in neither place and is lost forever. The claim must be atomic.

REASON FOR HAVING A "DONE" REDIS SET
    1. duplicates
    A REAPER CAN'T TELL DEAD FROM SLOW. It only sees “no lease renewal for 15 seconds.” 
    A worker stuck in a long GC pause, a CPU-starved container, or a delayed Redis connection looks 
    identical to a crashed one. The reaper requeues the chunk, a second worker runs it, and then the 
    first worker wakes up and finishes too. Both results land in queue:results. 

    This is the zombie case, and it's inherent to any lease-based system: you trade between 
    false positives (requeueing live work) and slow failure detection, but you can't eliminate both.

    2. Knowing when job is done

    Solution: Duplicate possibilities are inherently baked into the leasing approach. 
    So we can potentially have the same jobs done twice. We can prevent merging
    duplicates by using a redis set, and checking whether the finished job was already added


HEARTBEATS
    -coordinator looks through "inflight", and checks whether lease exists.
    -if lease does not exist, we assume worker has died and the job is requeued.
